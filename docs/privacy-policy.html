Privacy Policy

Fly Allergy Safe · Last updated 17 October 2026

Short version: We collect nothing. There is no account and no server, and everything you enter stays on your device. Google's ML Kit, which reads labels and translates, sends Google diagnostic data about how the app runs. Your allergens and profile are never given to it. Details below.

What we collect

Nothing. The app has no backend and no user accounts, and we have no way to see anything you enter.

What Google's ML Kit sends to Google

The app uses Google's ML Kit on your device to read text from label photos and to translate. According to Google's own disclosure, ML Kit sends Google diagnostic and usage data by default:

  • device information — manufacturer, model, iOS version
  • app information — the app's identifier and version
  • a per-installation identifier, which Google states is not intended to identify you or your device
  • performance measurements, such as how long a task took
  • which feature was used, configuration such as image size, and error codes
  • for translation, which languages are configured

Reading and translating both run entirely on your phone. Google's description of what ML Kit sends, at developers.google.com/ml-kit/ios-data-disclosure, does not include the text on a label or the text you translate, and nothing about your allergens, profiles or anything else you enter is ever passed to ML Kit at all.

In the App Store's terms, this data is declared under Identifiers (device ID), Usage Data (product interaction), Diagnostics, User Content (other user content) and Other Data, for analytics and app functionality. That list is copied from Google's own privacy declaration for ML Kit. None of it is linked to your identity, none of it is used to track you, and it goes to Google, not to us — we never receive it. There is currently no way for us to switch it off.

What stays on your device

All of it, in a local database that never leaves your phone:

  • Allergen profiles, severities and any custom allergens you define
  • Household profiles you create
  • Scanned products, scan history and saved items
  • Trips, flights, accommodation requests and incident reports you draft
  • Allergen exposures, flight experiences, seat cleaning logs, and the timeline recorded in emergency mode
  • Your "please don't feed my child" message
  • Auto-injector details, emergency contacts, your action plan and reaction logs

Deleting the app deletes all of it. We keep no copy, because we never had one.

When the app uses the network

In two situations, and only when you ask it to.

Looking up a product you scan. The app sends the barcode number — nothing else — to one or both of:

  • Open Food Facts, a non-profit open food database
  • USDA FoodData Central, a US government database

No profile, allergen, flight, location or identifier is ever attached to these requests. Results are cached on your device so the same product works offline afterwards.

Downloading a translation language. When you tap download on a language in Language packs, the app fetches that language's translation model — about 30 MB — from Google, through Google's ML Kit. This happens only when you tap download, over Wi-Fi by default. The request is for a language model; none of your text, profile or allergen data is sent with it. After the model is on your phone, translation runs entirely on the device.

Every other feature — the allergen matcher, the trip journal, emergency mode, voice input, chef cards, emergency guidance, and translation itself — works with no connection at all.

Analytics and tracking

We run no analytics of our own: no analytics SDK, no advertising identifier, no crash reporting service, and no tracking across apps or websites. The only diagnostic data that leaves the app is ML Kit's, described above, which goes to Google.

On-device intelligence

On supported iPhones the app uses Apple's on-device Foundation Models to fill in a form from what you said. This runs entirely on your phone. Nothing is sent to Apple or to us. On other devices the app uses built-in rules instead.

Camera

The camera is used to read barcodes and ingredient labels. You can also choose a photo of a label from your photo library; the app sees only the photo you pick. Images are read on your device and are not uploaded.

Microphone and speech recognition

The app listens only when you tap a microphone button, and while emergency mode is open (where you can switch it off). What you say is turned into text by Apple's speech recognition running on your iPhone; the app requires on-device recognition and does not offer voice on a phone that cannot do it. No audio is recorded, stored or sent to Apple or to us. The text fills in a form you review, or becomes a note in emergency mode.

Locking the app

On an iPhone with a passcode, the app asks for Face ID, Touch ID or that passcode when it opens and whenever you come back to it. This is on by default and can be turned off in Settings. It uses iOS's own unlock prompt: the app never sees your passcode or your biometric data, and stores one thing — whether you turned the lock on or off. For 30 minutes after you call or text for help from the app, it does not ask again; that is kept in memory only and never saved. The lock keeps the app closed; it is not encryption of the data on your device.

What you choose to share

Chef cards, incident reports, trip reports, emergency handover summaries and reaction histories can be shared through the normal iOS share sheet. That only happens when you tap share and pick where it goes. We are never a recipient.

Calling or messaging an emergency contact hands off to the iPhone's own Phone and Messages apps, with the number and any message text filled in. The app never places a call or sends a message on your behalf — you do that in those apps. Your contacts are stored on your device and are never uploaded.

Children

The app supports profiles for children so a parent or carer can manage them. Those profiles are ordinary local data and are treated no differently. Nothing is collected about anyone, of any age.
